# Digital-strength self-assessment rubric

The user requested a score; this replaces the earlier score-free 15-question flow with 12 questions. No scientific validation or industry comparison is claimed.

## Question roles

| Questions                                | Role                    | Weight                  |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------- |
| 1 industry, 2 stage                      | Context only            | 0                       |
| 3 website, 4 brand                       | Digital presence        | Equal capability weight |
| 5 discovery, 10 measurement              | Marketing & measurement | Equal capability weight |
| 6 lead follow-up, 7 customer experience  | Customer journey        | Equal capability weight |
| 8 connected systems, 9 useful automation | Systems & operations    | Equal capability weight |
| 11 priorities, 12 timing                 | Consultation context    | 0                       |

For each capability question, options run from 3 (consistently established), 2 (partly established), 1 (manual/inconsistent), 0 (not established). Not sure/not applicable is excluded from the denominator, not counted as failure. Four or more known capability answers are needed for an overall score. All 12 questions must be answered before showing it. Unknown answers remain available so people are not forced to invent information.

Score = round(sum of eligible points / (3 × known capability count) × 100). Category scores use the same formula within two relevant questions. Categories without known responses show insufficient context.

Bands are editorial interpretation of the rubric: <40 foundations to build; 40–69 progress with gaps; 70–100 strong foundations. They are not external benchmarks or promises of commercial performance. Business industry, size, age, budget and preferred timeline cannot penalize the score.

Recommendations identify below-maximum categories, ordered by score, with selected-answer evidence and related services. Strong results are not given fabricated problems. Unknown-heavy results offer clarification instead of a misleading number.

The client calculates a preview; the enquiry handler validates allowed answers and recalculates on the server. Client-provided scores are rejected as extra properties. Tests cover 0/100, 67/100, 100/100, context invariance, minimum known-answer coverage and incomplete data.
